# DRAWN TO RUIN — README LORE

*The surface kingdoms' version. What a literate, well-informed person
in a surface kingdom believes. It is the public story. It is not
true. It is close enough to true that nobody has caught the lie in a
thousand years.*

---

## The Buried City

There is a city under the mountain.

It was built by the Asteri — a civilization that existed before any
kingdom now standing, before any written history now kept. They were
one people in one place, and they were powerful. Their magic was not
our magic. Their Script was not our Script. What they could do with a
single card, our finest mages need a workshop and a week to
reproduce.

A thousand years ago, every Asteri vanished.

Not died. Vanished. There were no bodies. Their homes were left
exactly as they were, food still on tables, tools still in workshops,
doors still open. Nobody knows where they went. Nobody knows how. The
only survivor — the only one ever found — was a man at the bottom of
the city, standing beside a machine.

He was an Asteri. He was the one who built the machine. And he was the
one who used it.

Every account agrees on this: the researcher planned the
Disappearance. He was an evil man, and he took his own people for
power. He is still down there. He has been down there for a thousand
years, kept alive by the machine, still standing beside it, still
dangerous. Every explorer who has ever reached the chamber has
confirmed it. Every one of them has fought him. Most of them have
won.

None of them have come back up.

---

## The Gift We Inherited

The Asteri left their magic behind.

Not the knowledge — that died with them. But the raw material.
Script. It leaks out of their city in two forms: crystals dropped by
the strong monsters that have bred in the ruins, and fragments of
Asteri Script scattered through the districts.

We have learned to use both. Slowly. Painfully. We are a thousand
years behind them, and we know it.

There is something in the Asteri city that makes Script easier to
use. Every adventurer who has descended has felt it. You can cast
inside the ruins in ways you cannot cast outside them, regardless of
whether you were born with Script energy in your blood. The Asteri
must have built a **casting field** — some kind of ambient
enchantment that saturates the air with usable power.

If we could learn how they built it — reproduce it on the surface —
every kingdom's mages would be three times as strong overnight. This
is the great prize. Every expedition is, at bottom, a search for the
casting field.

We have been searching for a thousand years. We have not found it.
But we will.

---

## The Fragments

The Asteri Script fragments are the most valuable thing in the
ruins.

They are old. They are powerful. They are also, according to every
adventurer who has ever held one, **strange**.

Something happens when you carry a fragment. Other fragments call to
you. Not figuratively — literally. You sense them, the way you sense
your own hand. Direction. Distance. Presence. Adventurers describe it
as a second heartbeat in the back of the skull, only it isn't yours.

We do not know why. We do not know how. We have never been able to
study it because fragments are almost never recovered intact — the
monsters of the ruins seem drawn to them too, and most carriers die
before they can be studied.

What we do know is this: whoever holds a fragment can find the
others. This is why expeditions sometimes come back with two or
three fragments instead of one. It is why some adventurers have
reported "knowing" where to dig before they ever dug. It is why the
most successful explorers are the ones who found a fragment early
and did not die before they could use it.

Nobody has ever held a fragment and failed to sense the others. Not
once.

We do not know what happens if all fifteen are brought together. No
one has ever done it. Most who try die in the ruins. The few who
make it back with more than a couple fragments never go down again.

## The Bloodlines

Some people are born with Script energy in their blood.

They are the descendants of the few Asteri who left their homeland
long before the Disappearance and founded their own settlements
outside. Their blood remembers what their minds have forgotten. They
can cast outside the ruins, where everyone else cannot. They are
rare. They are valuable. They are, in most kingdoms, politically
significant.

Some kingdoms treat them as nobility. Some treat them as guild
members. Some treat them as tools. A few treat them as a resource to
be hoarded.

Every kingdom wants more of them. None of them can make more of
them. The bloodlines are finite, and the Asteri who founded them are
long gone.

---

## The Ruins

The Asteri city is not empty.

It is full of monsters. Some are the size of houses. Some are made of
things that should not be alive. They breed in the districts, nest in
the collapsed shafts, and grow stronger the deeper you go. They drop
crystals when they die, and the crystals are the only reliable source
of Script most adventurers ever see.

Every kingdom has an interest in the ruins. Every kingdom funds
expeditions. Every kingdom posts bounties. Every kingdom competes
with every other kingdom to be the one who finally cracks the city
open and brings home the casting field.

Some kingdoms call the city holy ground, blessed by a god or gods,
and forbid desecration. Some call it a vault of lost technology and
fund every expedition they can afford. Neither has ever come home
with the prize.

The city is still down there. It will still be down there when we are
gone.

## What We Know About the Researcher

He is the last Asteri. He is the reason the Asteri are gone. He is
dangerous, and he has been dangerous for a thousand years.

He is also the only being alive who remembers what the Asteri knew.
Every mage who has ever studied the ruins has dreamed of reaching him
and forcing the knowledge out of him. Nobody has ever managed it. He
fights. He always fights. He has been fighting for a thousand years
and he has not stopped.

Some say he is a demon. Some say he is a god. Some say he is just a
man who made one terrible choice and has been paying for it ever
since.

Most say: he is the final boss. Kill him and the city is ours.

Nobody has ever killed him and come back up to say so.

---

## The Church

There is an old religion. It has churches in most kingdoms. It has
priests and prayers and feast days and a founding text nobody can
fully translate.

It does not have a god.

Whatever the church once worshipped has been silent for a thousand
years. The modern church does not claim to hear it. The modern church
does not claim much of anything. It is a tradition, not a faith. It
is a building you go to for weddings and funerals and the occasional
festival.

Some scholars have tried to reconstruct what the church originally
believed. They have not gotten far. The founding text is in a
language older than Asteri Script, and nobody alive can read it.

Whatever the original church knew, it is gone.

---

## The War

There was a war. Every kingdom remembers it differently. Every
kingdom blames a different kingdom for starting it. Every kingdom
agrees on one thing: it was the dungeon's fault.

The monsters came out of the ruins. The monsters attacked the
surface. The kingdoms fought back. Millions died. The monsters were
pushed back into the mountain. The kingdoms rebuilt.

Some say the researcher sent the monsters. Some say the machine sent
them. Some say they have always been down there, and we just finally
woke them up.

Nobody has ever asked the question that matters: *who actually
started the war, and why.*

It is easier to blame the dungeon.

## The Explorer

There is nothing special about the person who goes down.

No prophecy. No chosen bloodline. No ancient mark on their hand. The
Asteri city does not care who you are. It kills kings and peasants
with the same indifference, and it always has.

The people who descend are ordinary. Bounty hunters. Debtors.
Scholars with too much curiosity and not enough sense. Soldiers who
ran out of wars. Third children of minor houses who need to make a
name or disappear trying. Every kingdom has them. Every kingdom
sends them down.

Most die on the first few floors. A few make it deeper. A very small
number reach the bottom.

The one who is going down now went down for the usual reasons. They
found something the usual way — by accident, by luck, by picking up
what someone else had dropped. They have a deck in their hand and a
bounty on their head and nothing else.

That is the whole of it. That is the whole of what makes them worth
following.

They are not the last hope of the world. They are just the next one
going down.

## How to Play (Short Version)

- **Draw five cards each turn.** Spend Energy to play them.
- **Attack, defend, enchant, chain.** Cards are the only resource —
  treat them carefully.
- **Monsters act on their own turn.** Read their intent, plan
  around it.
- **Rest sites** heal you or let you enchant a card.
- **Bosses** end each act. They are the dungeon's answer to you.
- **Descend. Survive. Come back up.**
