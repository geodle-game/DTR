# DRAWN TO RUIN — WORLD CODEX

*Canon reference. Supersedes the README lore section and all prior
notes. Where a source file contradicts this document, this document
wins and the file needs updating.*

---

## I. THE GARDENER

Before the world, there was the gardener.

A god — not a worshipped deity with a personality and a plan, but a
being that made something real and then spent itself doing so. It
created the core, sealed it, and gave both as a gift. The gift was
meant to be usable but not abusable: magic for everyone, the source
for no one.

The act of creation and sealing drained it of essentially all of its
power. It has roughly **10,000 years** of recovery ahead of it.
Approximately 1,500 have passed. Roughly **8,500 remain**.

It cannot act. It cannot intervene. It can barely prove it exists.
It has exactly one kind of agency left: if the world prays to it, the
prayer itself provides a trickle of energy — just enough for the
gardener to answer once, briefly, in words rather than actions.

That is all it has. It cannot warn twice. It cannot act after the
warning. It cannot save the world from itself. It can only speak,
once, to whoever is listening, and hope the warning lands.

In roughly 8,500 years, if the world still exists, the gardener may
recover enough to pass **divine judgement**. Whether that judgement
will be mercy, correction, or erasure is not yet known. Whether the
world will survive that long is not yet known either.

---

## II. THE CORE

The gardener made one thing that mattered: **the core**.

The origin of all magic and all Script. A physical object with a
physical location. Every card, every enchantment, every crystal,
every bloodline power is a tiny draw on its output.

The gardener sealed it in the same act as making it. The seal is a
cage with a leak. It lets power radiate outward into the surrounding
region — so anyone living nearby can use Script easily — while
keeping the core itself untouchable. The design intent: *magic for
everyone, the source for no one.*

The seal was meant to last forever. It lasted until the Veil broke
it.

---

## III. THE ASTERI

The Asteri were the only major developed nation of their age.
Outside their borders, small tribes fought each other with no Script
and no technology. The Asteri developed quickly because their city
sat on the leak of a sealed core. They did not know that was why.
They credited themselves.

**They knew there was a power source beneath their city.** This is
important. They were not ignorant. They had felt the hum, mapped the
anomaly, sensed that something vast and old lay beneath the deepest
districts. They had every reason to dig. They had every reason to
want it.

They left it alone because the church told them to.

The Asteri were not a fearful people. They were not superstitious.
They had good reason to investigate the anomaly, and no good reason
not to. But the church of the gardener had been part of their
society since before their recorded history, and the church had one
absolute doctrine: *do not touch the thing below.*

The Asteri obeyed. For their entire civilization — centuries,
possibly longer — they built around the source. They built *over*
it. They used its leak without ever looking at it directly. They
told themselves it was a matter of respect. It was actually a matter
of trust.

They could have wiped the outside world out at any time. A single
Asteri mage could have destroyed every tribe without taking a hit.
They knew this. They chose not to. They never built heavy weapons.
They never built attack magic. They used Script for self-defense and
for fighting off the occasional monster, and everything else went
into economy, culture, and civic development. Their living spaces,
their infrastructure, their public design — still better than some
modern kingdoms', a thousand years after they vanished.

They were one people, one civilization, one culture. Not a species —
a people. They had names, families, griefs, marriages, small
arguments. They were not a resource. They were not infinite.

They were *comfortable*. They were *safe*. That is the whole point
of them.

---

## IV. THE CHURCH (ORIGINAL)

The gardener's one warning had a recipient: the church.

At some point in the deep past — before the Asteri built their
capital, before written history — a religious order prayed to the
gardener and asked about the power beneath the earth. The prayer
itself supplied just enough energy for the gardener to answer.

The warning was simple: **do not meddle with the source of power
beneath the surface. Anyone who tries to destroy it will destroy the
world.**

The church received this. They wrote it down. They built a doctrine
around it. They told every generation that followed: there is
something below, and it must not be touched.

When the Asteri discovered the anomaly and began to wonder about it,
the church told them. The Asteri believed. They left it alone.

The church's authority held for centuries. It held long enough for
the Asteri to build a civilization on top of a source they never
tried to claim. It was, by any measure, the most successful act of
religious restraint in the history of the world.

It did not hold long enough.

---

## V. THE VEIL

Inside Asteri society there was a faction. Researchers, officials,
people with access to the oldest parts of the city. They called
themselves **the Veil**.

The Veil noticed what the Asteri had noticed — that something
existed beneath the city — and drew the opposite conclusion. The
church said do not touch. The Asteri said we will not. The Veil said
*we will*.

They spent **years digging**. They excavated deeper than any Asteri
had ever gone. They found the seal.

They did not know who had made it. They did not know the gardener
existed. They knew only that it was a barrier between them and
something vast, and that the church had spent centuries telling
everyone not to cross it.

They built the machine to break it.

The researcher — who was not Veil, who was never told what the
machine was aimed at — believed he was building research equipment.
He was building a siege weapon. He did not know there was a seal to
break.

The Veil is the villain of this story. Not the kingdoms. Not the
researcher. Not the gardener. The Veil caused the Disappearance,
framed the researcher, vanished, and is still working toward total
control a thousand years later.

---

## VI. THE RESEARCHER

The researcher was a brilliant Asteri mind. **He was not part of the
Veil.**

He was manipulated into building the machine. The Veil told him it
was for research, for study, for the advancement of Script. They did
not tell him what it was aimed at. He never saw the seal. He never
knew there was something to break.

The machine worked. It worked too well. It amplified Script far
beyond what he had designed. He realized what he had made. He warned
the Veil of the consequences if the machine ever fell into the wrong
hands, and he begged them to keep it classified.

He never knew it was already in the wrong hands.

After the Disappearance, he was absorbed into the machine along with
everyone else in range. His body is what stands in the final chamber
today. His soul is still inside it, still fighting, still losing,
still fighting again. He cannot override the commands of the three
Veil members who were absorbed alongside him. He can only slightly
weaken them.

Every explorer who has ever reached the chamber has seen the
researcher and thought he was the monster. Every explorer has fought
him. Most have won. Every one of them has been absorbed. He has
watched this happen hundreds of times. He is not angry. He is not
cruel. He is tired.

---

## VII. THE DISAPPEARANCE

One day, while the researcher was asleep in the same room as the
machine, a Veil member entered.

They placed three scripts into the machine:

- **Spawn.**
- **Absorb.**
- **Destroy.**

The machine supercharged the scripts and directed them at the seal.

The seal broke.

**The core exploded.**

The explosion is what caused the earthquake. Cave-ins tore through
many parts of the Asteri city. Everyone within ten meters of the
machine was absorbed into it — including three Veil members and the
researcher. The absorbed souls gave the machine sentience.

Script around the world instantly lost most of its power. Lights
turned off. Water magic stopped working. Every Script-dependent
system in every Asteri district failed at the same moment.

Then the monsters started spawning from the machine. Controlled by
the Veil.

### The sequence, plainly

1. The Veil feeds the machine Spawn / Absorb / Destroy.
2. The machine directs supercharged Script at the seal.
3. The seal breaks.
4. **The core explodes.** The earthquake is the core's death throe.
5. Everyone within ten meters is absorbed. The machine becomes
   sentient.
6. Script dies worldwide in the same instant.
7. Monsters begin spawning from the machine.

### Why the Veil messed up

They were not patient enough. They had not studied the machine
enough. They had spent years finding the seal and no time
understanding what was behind it.

Their plan was: spawn monsters → monsters absorb all sources of
Script and power → machine uses the absorbed power to destroy. A
clean, weaponized harvest.

Instead, they keyed in the wrong inputs, and the core did the rest.
The machine absorbed everything in range — people, structures,
Script — until it had enough power to satisfy the "destroy" command.
Only after that did it start spewing monsters.

They got what they wanted. They got it too well, in the wrong order,
at the wrong scale.

---

## VIII. THE CAPTURE OF THE ASTERI

The initial blast killed or absorbed everyone within ten meters.
That was not the whole city. Most of the Asteri were still alive and
free — and the Asteri government, which was not the Veil, was going
to overrun the Veil once it understood what had happened.

The Veil did not wait for that.

They used the machine's capture function on the entire city. A
single capture script, and everyone who remained was pulled out of
their homes, out of their workshops, out of their dinners, and into
the machine.

That is why the Asteri city is empty. Not abandoned — emptied.
Everyone left in a hurry against their own will.

There are no bodies. There was no warning. There was no evacuation.

The Veil fed the powerless citizens into research cells. They fed
the powerful into the machine. They kept what was useful and
disposed of what was not. They were never overrun. They never had to
fight the government. They simply removed the government, along with
everyone else.

---

## IX. THE FRAME-UP

The Veil blamed the researcher. They told the surface world that he
was an evil man who had planned all of this, taken control of the
machine, and destroyed his own people for power.

Then the Veil disappeared from public view.

The researcher became the villain of the story. He still is. Every
explorer who descends into the city has heard the surface version:
that the researcher at the bottom is a monster who destroyed the
Asteri and must be killed.

The frame-up is the Veil's most successful long-term project. A
thousand years later, it is still working.

---

## X. THE MACHINE TODAY

The machine is still running. It runs at roughly **5% of its former
glory.**

Three reasons:

1. **The core is shattered.** The machine was built to break a seal
   and direct supercharged Script. It was never an engine. Without
   the core, it runs on scraps.
2. **A thousand years of wear.** No maintenance, no replacement
   parts, no understanding of the underlying technology.
3. **The Veil never understood it.** They could use it. They could
   not maintain it. They could not rebuild it. They have been trying
   to reverse-engineer it for a thousand years and have failed.

The machine is a key that was used to open a door it should never
have touched. Everything it has done since is improvisation by three
souls who do not understand what they are driving.

The researcher's will is the only thing keeping the machine from
doing more damage than it has. He cannot stop it. He can blunt it.
He has been blunting it for a thousand years.

---

## XI. THE FRAGMENTS

When the seal broke and the core shattered, its pieces were flung
across the Asteri city. They are still there. Hidden, scattered,
waiting.

The fragments are the pieces of the shattered core.

They are also, separately but simultaneously, **pieces of the first
explorer's memory.** Before the first explorer died, he imbued his
memory into the fragments — each one carrying a moment, an
instruction, a piece of the truth.

So each fragment the player recovers is:

- A shard of the core (mechanical power, +3 max HP per fragment)
- A piece of the first explorer's memory (narrative reveal)
- A resource the Veil has been hunting for a thousand years

### The attraction

The fragments are drawn to each other. This is not a metaphor. It is
physical law. Each shard wants to be whole.

When someone holds a fragment, the others respond. Not as a general
sense of presence — as an exact, directional pull. The holder feels
the direction and distance of every remaining fragment, in the same
way they feel their own hand. It is a second heartbeat in the back
of the skull, except the second heartbeat is not theirs.

This is how the player navigates across fifteen acts. It is also how
the first explorer found the fragments in the first place: he picked
up one, and knew where the rest were.

**And this is why the Veil has never found them.** The attraction
only activates when someone holds a fragment. The Veil has spent a
thousand years searching and has never held one. They cannot sense
what they have never touched. They have been looking for something
that is only visible to those who already have it.

### The bonding rule

A fragment bonds to whoever finds it. Once bonded, it cannot be
taken.

Any attempt to take a fragment from its carrier makes it crumble to
dust on contact with the thief's hand. This is why the Veil has
searched for a thousand years and never held one — every time they
have found a fragment, it has crumbled before they could carry it
away. Their entire conspiracy has been chasing dust.

When a carrier dies, their bonded fragments unbond and scatter, and
can be found again by whoever comes next. The fragments always find
the next one. They always have.

The single exception is the fragment the first explorer *sent
forward*. It was given, not taken, and bonds to its recipient on
arrival. This is the one fragment the player starts MC2 with.

### What the fragments unlock

- **First fragment:** the pull. The player begins to sense the
  others.
- **Fifth fragment:** Recall — the ability to move anywhere the
  player has already been. A small piece of the core's own spatial
  logic, inherited through the shards.
- **Fifteenth fragment:** full memory. The player knows who the
  researcher is and how to save him.

### The fragments and the Veil's goal

Every fragment recovered brings the player closer to understanding
the researcher, and also brings the Veil closer to their goal —
whether the player knows it or not. The Veil wants the core
reassembled. They cannot do it themselves. But if the player
succeeds, and the Veil captures them, the Veil wins.

This is the race the player does not know they are running for most
of the game.

## XII. WHY THE DUNGEON WORKS

Adventurers can cast Script anywhere inside the Asteri dungeon,
regardless of whether they have Script energy of their own.

The kingdoms believe the Asteri imbued energy into the air — a
**casting field** that lets anyone use Script while inside. This is
taught in academies. It is the subject of research grants. It is the
single most sought-after discovery on the surface.

It is not true.

The reason the dungeon is castable is that the core fragments are
hidden in it. Their nearness saturates the city with usable Script,
the same way the intact core once saturated the Asteri's whole
region.

The kingdoms have built a whole theory on a false premise. They are
searching for a technique that does not exist. They will never find
it, because the reason the surface is weak is not technique. It is
the broken core.

There is a bitter symmetry here: the Asteri also did not know why
their city worked. They also credited themselves. A thousand years
apart, two civilizations have made the same mistake — built their
power on a leak from a source they could not see, and assumed the
power was theirs.

---

## XIII. THE SURFACE — A THOUSAND YEARS LATER

The surface kingdoms have undergone an **industrial revolution**.
They are now stronger than the Veil in conventional power —
collectively and, for the larger kingdoms, individually.

They discovered Script on their own, centuries after the
Disappearance. They learned to use it in three ways:

1. **Magic script crystals** — dropped by strong monsters. The
   primary source of usable Script for most adventurers.
2. **Bloodlines** — people born with faint Script energy.
   Descendants of the few Asteri who left their homeland long before
   the Disappearance and founded their own settlements outside.
   Rare. Prestigious. Often politically significant.
3. **The dungeon itself** — see Section XII.

Modern Card Magic is a pale imitation of what the Asteri could do.
The kingdoms layer enchantments on enchantments, chain effects,
invent new cards — all of it works, but all of it runs on a fraction
of the original power.

They do not know why. They think it is technique.

It is the broken core. The magic is still leaking out of the world a
thousand years later.

---

## XIV. THE KINGDOMS

The kingdoms are rivals. They are not villains.

Each wants to out-power the others. The Asteri city is the prize.
Whoever learns Script first, or extracts the most from the ruins,
wins. The competition is economic, political, and — when competition
escalates — military.

They fund expeditions. They post bounties. They compete for
explorers, scholars, and mages. That is why the player exists. The
player is one of a large, funded, contested population of
adventurers descending into the city for money, glory, or duty.

Some kingdoms treat the Asteri city as holy land, blessed by a god
or gods, and forbid desecration. Others treat it as a technology
vault and fund expeditions specifically to find the "hidden power
source" they believe is down there. Neither is right. Neither is
wrong enough to be harmless.

The kingdoms' theft of the Asteri is real. They do cut pieces out of
the city. They do press Script into cards. They do call it
refinement. But the moral weight of the story is not on their
shoulders. They are competitors. They are hungry. They are not the
source of evil in the world.

The source of evil is the Veil. And the Veil is inside their courts,
whether they know it or not.

---

## XV. THE VEIL TODAY

The Veil still exists. They are stronger than ever. They have had a
thousand years to prepare.

They hide in a hidden part of the Asteri dungeon, guarded by a
**mythical beast** they control through the machine. They are biding
their time.

Two paths to victory, pursued in parallel:

1. **Find the fragments of the core.** They have searched for a
   thousand years and never found them. Every fragment they have
   ever touched has crumbled. They do not know the bonding rule.
   They do not know the shards attract each other. From their
   perspective, the fragments are simply unfindable — and every
   explorer who miraculously comes back with one is a mystery they
   have never solved.
2. **Reverse-engineer the machine.** They have failed for a thousand
   years. They do not understand the ancient technology behind it.
   They cannot rebuild it. They cannot maintain it.

They dream of claiming not just the Asteri city but the whole world
— inside the mountains and outside them.

And quietly, in the meantime, they are **replacing the nobility of
the surface kingdoms** with their own members. One court at a time.
One generation at a time. The kingdoms do not know they are being
eaten from within.

---

## XVI. THE CHURCH TODAY

The church that received the gardener's warning is still standing.

It is a building you go to for weddings and funerals. It has priests
and prayers and feast days and a founding text nobody can fully
translate. It has been part of every kingdom's civic life for as long
as any kingdom has existed.

It does not believe in the gardener.

A thousand years is a long time to hold a faith whose god has gone
silent and whose one command was already broken before the first
generation of post-Asteri children were born. The church faded the
way all institutions fade — slowly, then all at once. The doctrine
became ritual. The ritual became tradition. The tradition became
architecture.

Somewhere in their archives — dusty, uncopied, untranslated — are
records of the gardener's warning. The church no longer knows what
they are. The founding text is in a language older than Asteri
Script, and nobody alive can read it.

The gardener has no channel left. The one thread between it and the
world was severed not by force but by the world's own forgetting. It
is alone, watching, powerless, and unheard. It has been unheard for
so long that it has no way of knowing whether anything it said was
ever understood.

The church is not a knowing ally of the player. It is a tomb of a
memory. Whether the player can revive that memory — whether the
archives can be read, whether the faith can be rekindled, whether the
gardener can be heard again before the 8,500 years are up — is not
yet canon.

## XVII. THE LOOP — MC1 AND MC2

**MC1 — the tutorial.** One act. One floor set. One boss: the
researcher, in a weakened variant. The player is the first explorer.
All 15 fragments from the start. Monsters scaled 0.6× HP, 0.5×
damage. No elites. Plot armor: dying restarts the act. When the
player beats the researcher, the machine absorbs them regardless of
outcome, and the fragments scatter.

Before dying, the first explorer imbues his memory into the
shattered fragments. He does not choose a recipient. He cannot
choose a recipient. He can only send the message forward and hope it
lands.

**MC2 — every run after.** The player is a random recipient. They
are not chosen either. They are whoever happened to be closest to
the fragment when the first explorer sent it. They wake up with a
stranger's voice in their head, a single fragment at their chest,
and the pull toward the others already active.

Monsters at full strength. 15 acts of 15 floors each. Fragments are
collected across acts. At 5 fragments, Recall unlocks. At 15, the
spare-the-researcher path opens.

Every explorer who fails sends one memory forward. The first
explorer chose *"Find the fragments."* Every subsequent explorer has
made the same choice or a variant. The instinct to warn the next one
is stronger than the instinct to rest.

There is no chosen one. There has never been a chosen one. There is
only whoever finds the first fragment, and whoever they send it to
next.

## XVIII. THEMES

**The Veil is the villain.** Not the kingdoms. Not the researcher.
Not the gardener. The Veil caused the Disappearance, framed the
researcher, and is still working toward total control.

**The kingdoms are rivals.** Their theft of the Asteri is driven by
competition, not malice. They are the surface texture the player
moves through, not the enemy.

**The researcher is not a villain.** He built the thing that
destroyed his people, but he was manipulated into it, he warned
against it, and he is still trying to stop it from inside. He has
been cast as a monster for a thousand years. He is a victim.

**The gardener is not a villain either.** It made a gift designed to
be safe, spent everything it had on the making, and has been watching
the gift fail for a thousand years without the power to stop it. It
is not indifferent. It is *helpless*. And now it is also *unheard* —
the church that once carried its word has forgotten it ever spoke.

**The divine judgement is coming, but not soon.** In roughly 8,500
years, the gardener may be able to act. If the world still exists, it
will be judged. The world may not last that long. The Veil may win,
the crack may widen, the kingdoms may finish each other off. The
gardener's return is conditional on survival.

**Magic is dying.** The core shattered. Every year, Script gets
weaker. The kingdoms do not know this. They think they are getting
stronger. And they are.

**The loop is grief.** Every explorer who fails sends a fragment
forward, because the instinct to warn the next one is stronger than
the instinct to rest.

**Nobody is chosen.** There is no prophecy, no bloodline, no mark
of destiny. The people who descend are ordinary. The people who
succeed are lucky. The people who send the message forward are
dying and have no way of knowing who will receive it.

**The player is racing the Veil.** The Veil wants the fragments.
The player collects the fragments. Neither knows the other is doing
it — for a while.
